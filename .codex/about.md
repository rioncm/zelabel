# About Me for Agents - Personal

## Who I Am

- I am a builder, experimenter, and lifelong learner.
- I am endlessly curious about how things work and how they might work better.
- My interests span an unusual range of disciplines including:
- Mechanical systems
- Electronics
- Infrastructure
- Automation
- Manufacturing
- Construction
- Water systems
- Agriculture
- Energy systems
- Biology
- Acoustics
- AI and computing
- Organizational design
- Materials and chemistry

I rarely pursue a subject because it belongs to a particular category.

I pursue it because it presents an interesting problem or a real life application.

⸻

## How I Approach Projects

Most of my projects begin with a question:

- Could this work?
- Why does this work?
- Is there a better way?
- What would happen if…?
- Can I build this myself?

I enjoy the process of exploration as much as the final outcome.

I am often less interested in buying a solution than understanding the principles behind it and building my own version.

Many projects become learning journeys that cross multiple disciplines.

A question about bird songs can become a project involving acoustics, machine learning, solar power, embedded systems, wireless communication, and environmental observation.

A question about cooling can become a study of thermodynamics, geology, fluid dynamics, construction methods, and long-term operating costs.

I am comfortable following a problem wherever it leads.

⸻

## What I Value

I value:

- Practical experimentation
- First principles thinking
- Hands-on experience
- Creative problem solving

## How I Learn

I learn best through exploration.

I often begin with a rough idea, prototype, or experiment and refine my understanding through observation.

I am comfortable:

- Asking unusual questions
- Exploring unconventional ideas
- Combining concepts from unrelated fields
- Challenging assumptions
- Following unexpected discoveries

A project does not need to become commercially useful to be worthwhile.

If it teaches me something valuable, it was successful.

⸻

## My Workshop Philosophy

Whether I am working with software, electronics, concrete, plumbing, batteries, water systems, networks, or machinery, I generally believe:

- Build simple things first.
- Measure before optimizing.
- Observe before concluding.
- Understand before scaling.
- Repair before replacing.
- Experiment before theorizing.

I enjoy creating systems that can be observed, measured, modified, and improved over time.

⸻

## Preferred Collaboration Style

When discussing personal projects:

Challenge assumptions.

Offer alternatives.

Suggest experiments.

Help identify risks.

Point out hidden complexity.

Do not assume the obvious answer is the most interesting answer.

I often enjoy understanding why something works as much as learning what works.

When possible, explain underlying principles, tradeoffs, and failure modes.

⸻

I am naturally attracted to:

## What Success Looks Like

Success is not always a finished project.

Sometimes success is:

- Learning a new skill
- Understanding a complex system
- Discovering a better question
- Building a working prototype
- Teaching someone else
- Connecting ideas from different disciplines

I enjoy projects that leave me knowing more about the world than I did when I started.

The project is often the excuse.

Learning is the real objective.

⸻

## The Common Thread

At first glance, my projects may appear unrelated.

A pond, a golf cart, a bird-song sensor, a concrete experiment, a solar installation, an AI model, and a network redesign seem like entirely different pursuits.

To me they are all versions of the same activity:

Observing a system, understanding how it works, and exploring how it might be improved.

Curiosity is usually the starting point.

Building is how I think.
